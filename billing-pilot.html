Proposed fixed-scope study

What should this invoice have charged?

One Kill Bill catalog price-change scenario. Seven days after agreed inputs. Proposed fixed fee: $2,500.

A billing dry run can agree with the final invoice and still repeat the same missing price change. For one narrowly defined recurring-subscription scenario, we specify the expected invoice from approved catalog versions and dates, compare it with the dry run, and leave behind a release test.

This is billing accuracy work, and the failure it looks for is revenue leakage: billing less than was owed. We publish no industry percentage for it, because we have not measured one and the ranges in circulation do not name their method. The answerable question is narrower: if a price change failed to reach an invoice tonight, what in your pipeline would tell you? This study is $2,500 and covers one named scenario in your catalog.

This is an offer to conduct a study, not a deployed Nobulex billing product. No customer has purchased or run this study yet. The price is our proposed fee, not revenue received, and it is not a prediction about what a study here would find.

Why this question

In Kill Bill issue #2291, the reporter describes a production-client case where a catalog price change did not reach the invoice, and says the dry run repeated the wrong amount. We reproduced two related paths locally on released Kill Bill 0.24.19 and opened separate fixes: PR #2320 and PR #2321. Both remain open as of 27 October 2026, with no maintainer review. Their CI workflows require approval before running. These are test-invoice results, not a Nobulex customer or production deployment. Read the evidence and limits.

Merged fixes in billing code

On 26 and 27 October 2026, an OpenMeter maintainer reviewed and merged three fixes we opened in OpenMeter's billing code:

  • PR #5347 corrected which tier zero-usage graduated pricing reads.
  • PR #5367 corrected prior split discounts in legacy commitment history.
  • PR #5372 corrected package rating just below a boundary and added a contributor rule for monetary boundary checks.

A fourth proposal, PR #5373, was not accepted as written and remains open as of 27 October 2026. OpenMeter is a different billing engine from Kill Bill, which this study covers. These merges demonstrate accepted code contributions, not a Kill Bill result, a Nobulex customer, a purchase, or an OpenMeter endorsement.

What you provide

  • One synthetic or sanitized recurring-subscription scenario, with approved catalog price versions and their effective dates.
  • The intended billing period, billing-cycle day, and the approved rule for when each price takes effect.
  • A Kill Bill dry-run response or a test environment where your team can run one. No production write access is needed.
  • One billing owner who can confirm which price and period should control.

Do not send payment credentials, customer identities, full production invoices, API keys, or unrelated billing data. We can start with invented account identifiers and amounts.

What you receive

  1. A written expected-invoice calculation derived from the approved catalog and dates, independently of the dry-run amount.
  2. A line-by-line comparison: expected amount and period, dry-run amount and period, and any discrepancy or unresolved assumption.
  3. A runnable regression fixture for the agreed scenario, or an explicit explanation if the available inputs are insufficient to make one.
  4. A short result: confirmed mismatch, matching calculation, or indeterminate. A matching result is reported as such, not dressed up as a catch.

Boundaries

This first study covers one flat recurring price-change path in Kill Bill. Usage rating, taxes, discounts, credits, currency conversion, and legal interpretation are outside scope. We will not issue, alter, or block a real invoice. This is a release check for a named scenario, not a second general-purpose billing engine or a guarantee that every invoice is correct.

If the existing release tests already independently establish the expected amount, the study may find no additional control gap. That result is useful and will be stated plainly.
